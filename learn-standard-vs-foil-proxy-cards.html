Standard vs Foil Proxy Cards

The two finishes look different at a glance, but the real differences go deeper than shine.

The materials are genuinely different, not just the finish

Standard cards print on Epson Premium Presentation Matte — a stock built to resist fading for decades, so the colors on a standard card stay accurate long after a cheaper print would start to shift.

Foil cards print on holographic vinyl instead of paper stock. That's real, printed holographic material, not a foil stamp pressed on afterward — and because it's vinyl, it's also waterproof, which a paper-based card never will be. Foil fronts get mounted to a 199gsm core afterward so the finished card still has proper weight and stiffness, not just a shiny surface on something flimsy.

What that means for how each one feels

Standard cards feel closest to a normal printed card — flat matte finish, no glare, easy to read text and art at any angle. Foil cards catch light and shift with movement the way a real foil or holo card does, which is the whole point for anyone chasing that look for a favorite card or a custom build.

Every card, foil or standard, gets an anti-reflective semi-gloss back, so backs stay sharp and glare-free either way — the difference is entirely on the front.

Which one to pick

If the goal is something that plays and reads cleanly at the table, or a faithful recreation of a non-foil card, standard is usually the right call. If the goal is a foil, holo, or alt-art recreation — or just a card that catches the eye — foil is worth the extra step.
